# Product

## Register

product

## Users

GymFlow is for people who train consistently and need one system for both the gym floor and the planning that happens around it. Mobile is the fast, tactile companion for logging sets during a session. Desktop is the richer workspace for preparing workouts, reading training patterns, and reviewing past sessions.

## Product Purpose

GymFlow turns a workout library and completed sessions into a usable training system. The desktop experience should help a person decide what to train next, prepare the session with confidence, and understand their consistency and volume before they step into the gym. Starting a workout remains possible everywhere, but the interface should make each device's strengths obvious.

## Brand Personality

Focused, athletic, and precise. The product should carry the performance clarity of Strava and Whoop without becoming a noisy sports feed or a generic SaaS dashboard. It should feel premium through useful density, sharp hierarchy, and composed energy.

## Anti-references

- A stretched mobile layout with oversized controls and unused desktop space
- Generic KPI dashboards with decorative charts or invented performance data
- Neon gaming aesthetics, bodybuilding clichés, and aggressive visual noise
- Interfaces that force people into a device-specific workflow when a useful action can be available on both

## Design Principles

1. Use each screen size for its strength: execute quickly on mobile; prepare and understand on desktop.
2. Make real training signals legible before adding decoration.
3. Keep the next useful action close, while preserving access to the full workout flow.
4. Treat workout templates as plans that can be reviewed and improved, not just buttons that launch a session.
5. Use energy deliberately: active states and progress should feel motivating without competing with the data.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast and keyboard-navigation expectations. Every data visualization needs a text alternative or accessible label, and motion must remain optional through reduced-motion preferences.
